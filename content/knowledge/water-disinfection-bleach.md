# Disinfecting drinking water with household bleach (official guidance)

Curated quick reference. Use these numbers over older sources in the library.
Checked against the agency pages on 2026-11-18.

## Which bleach

- Regular, unscented liquid chlorine bleach (sodium hypochlorite). Not scented, not color-safe, no added cleaners.
- Read the strength on the label. In the US it is usually 5% to 9% (often 6% or 8.25%).

## How much, per gallon of clear water

| Bleach strength | Per gallon | Per quart / liter | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6% | 8 drops | 2 drops | EPA |
| 8.25% | 6 drops | 2 drops | EPA |
| 5% to 9% | 8 drops (a little less than 1/8 teaspoon, about 1/2 mL) | — | CDC |
| 1% | 40 drops (1/2 teaspoon) | 10 drops | CDC |

- Cloudy, colored or very cold water: use double the amount. If it is cloudy, first let it settle and draw off the clear water, or filter it through a clean cloth, paper towel or coffee filter (CDC).

## Then

1. Stir and let stand at least 30 minutes.
2. The water should have a slight chlorine smell. If not, repeat the dose and wait another 15 minutes (EPA).
3. If the taste is too strong, pour it between clean containers and let it stand a few hours (EPA).

## Older figures you may find in the library

The 2004 FEMA "Are You Ready?" guide says "16 drops (1/8 teaspoon) per gallon" for 5.25–6% bleach. Copies of that text appear on Appropedia and in several LibreTexts chemistry pages. It is outdated: current EPA and CDC guidance is 8 drops per gallon for ordinary bleach.
